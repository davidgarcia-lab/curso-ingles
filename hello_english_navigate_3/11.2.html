¡Me encanta este tema! Las noticias pueden ser serias, impactantes o preocupantes, pero también pueden ser inspiradoras. A veces una pequeña buena acción se convierte en un hermoso headline.

News Vocabulary

Exactly. Let’s begin with news vocabulary. A “headline” is the title of a news story. A “robbery” is when someone steals using force or threats. “Survival” means staying alive after danger. A “victory” is a win. A “cure” is something that makes an illness better. “Conflict” means serious disagreement or fighting.

Exactamente. Empecemos con vocabulario de noticias. Un “headline” es el título de una noticia. Un “robbery” es cuando alguien roba usando fuerza o amenazas. “Survival” significa mantenerse vivo después de un peligro. Una “victory” es un triunfo. Una “cure” es algo que mejora una enfermedad. “Conflict” significa desacuerdo serio o pelea.

And for positive news, we can use adjectives like “inspiring,” “encouraging,” “uplifting,” and “remarkable.”

Y para noticias positivas, podemos usar adjetivos como “inspiring,” “encouraging,” “uplifting,” y “remarkable”.
  • For example: The rescue was inspiring.Por ejemplo: El rescate fue inspiring.
  • The new medical discovery is encouraging.El nuevo descubrimiento médico es encouraging.
  • The community project was uplifting.El proyecto comunitario fue uplifting.
  • Her survival was remarkable.Su survival fue remarkable.

For negative news, we may use words like “unfortunate,” “disturbing,” “depressing,” and “violent.”

Para noticias negativas, podemos usar palabras como “unfortunate,” “disturbing,” “depressing,” y “violent”.
  • For example: The accident was unfortunate.Por ejemplo: El accidente fue unfortunate.
  • The report was disturbing.El informe fue disturbing.
  • The story was depressing.La historia fue depressing.
  • The robbery was violent.El robbery fue violent.

Reading and Listening Story

Yesterday morning, a local newspaper reported three positive stories from the city. The first story was about farmers and supermarket buyers. For years, supermarkets refused to buy fruit and vegetables that looked unusual.

Ayer por la mañana, un periódico local reportó tres historias positivas de la ciudad. La primera historia era sobre agricultores y compradores de supermercados. Durante años, los supermercados se negaron a comprar frutas y verduras que se veían inusuales.

Carrots with two legs, small apples, and strange tomatoes were often thrown away. A group of farmers argued that this was a waste of good food. They pointed out that unusual fruit tasted the same as perfect-looking fruit.

Zanahorias con dos piernas, manzanas pequeñas y tomates extraños a menudo se tiraban. Un grupo de agricultores argued que esto era un desperdicio de buena comida. Ellos pointed out que la fruta inusual sabía igual que la fruta de apariencia perfecta.

After several meetings, supermarket buyers admitted that customers were ready for a change. They announced that they would sell “funny fruit boxes” at lower prices. By lunchtime, every box had sold out.

Después de varias reuniones, los compradores de supermercados admitted que los clientes estaban listos para un cambio. Ellos announced que venderían “funny fruit boxes” a precios más bajos. Para la hora del almuerzo, todas las cajas se habían vendido.

The second story came from a wildlife park. Conservationists had warned that a family of rare monkeys might lose their home because of construction work nearby. Local people promised to help.

La segunda historia vino de un parque de vida silvestre. Los conservacionistas habían warned que una familia de rare monkeys podría perder su hogar debido a trabajos de construcción cercanos. La gente local promised ayudar.

Students organized information posters, teachers encouraged families to visit the park, and a group of volunteers offered to plant new trees. Finally, the town council confirmed that the construction would move to another area. The conservationists assured everyone that the monkeys would be safe.

Los estudiantes organizaron carteles informativos, los profesores encouraged a las familias to visit el parque, y un grupo de voluntarios offered to plant nuevos árboles. Finalmente, el consejo municipal confirmed que la construcción se trasladaría a otra área. Los conservacionistas assured a todos que los monos estarían seguros.

The third story was about two teenagers on a train. They spotted a black backpack on an empty seat. At first, they thought someone had forgotten school books, but inside the bag there was a large amount of money.

La tercera historia era sobre dos adolescentes en un tren. Vieron una mochila negra en un asiento vacío. Al principio, pensaron que alguien había olvidado libros escolares, pero dentro de la bolsa había una gran cantidad de dinero.

They immediately informed the police. Later, the owner explained that he had been taking the money to pay workers at his small business. The police praised the teenagers for acting honestly, and the man thanked them warmly.

Ellos informed inmediatamente a la policía. Más tarde, el dueño explained que había estado llevando el dinero para pagar a los trabajadores de su pequeño negocio. La policía praised a los adolescentes for acting honestamente, y el hombre les agradeció cálidamente.

He said their kindness had saved his business from a terrible problem.

Él dijo que su amabilidad había salvado su negocio de un problema terrible.

Grammar: Reporting Verbs

Now let’s move to grammar: reporting verbs. Instead of always saying “said,” we can use more specific verbs.

Ahora pasemos a la gramática: reporting verbs. En lugar de decir siempre “said,” podemos usar verbos más específicos.
  • For example: “The farmers argued that it was a waste.”Por ejemplo: “The farmers argued that it was a waste.”
  • “The buyers admitted that customers wanted change.”“The buyers admitted that customers wanted change.”
  • “The newspaper reported that the boxes had sold out.”“The newspaper reported that the boxes had sold out.”

Different reporting verbs use different structures. Some verbs are followed by “that” plus a clause: announce, admit, claim, confirm, deny, explain, point out, report, and suggest.

Diferentes reporting verbs usan diferentes estructuras. Algunos verbos van seguidos de “that” más una cláusula: announce, admit, claim, confirm, deny, explain, point out, report y suggest.
  • Examples: The council confirmed that the monkeys were safe.Ejemplos: El consejo confirmed that los monos estaban seguros.
  • The owner explained that the money was important.El dueño explained that el dinero era importante.
  • The newspaper reported that the teenagers had called the police.El periódico reported that los adolescentes habían llamado a la policía.

Some verbs are followed by a person and “that.” For example: remind and assure.

Algunos verbos van seguidos de una persona y “that.” Por ejemplo: remind y assure.
  • The conservationists assured local people that the animals were safe.Los conservacionistas assured a la gente local that los animales estaban seguros.
  • The teacher reminded students that small actions could help the community.El profesor reminded a los estudiantes that las pequeñas acciones podían ayudar a la comunidad.

Some reporting verbs are followed by “to” plus infinitive. “Agree,” “offer,” “promise,” and “refuse” work like this.

Algunos reporting verbs van seguidos de “to” más infinitivo. “Agree,” “offer,” “promise,” y “refuse” funcionan así.
  • The volunteers offered to plant trees.Los voluntarios offered to plant árboles.
  • The students promised to help.Los estudiantes promised to help.
  • The supermarket agreed to sell unusual fruit.El supermercado agreed to sell fruta inusual.
  • At first, some buyers refused to buy strange vegetables.Al principio, algunos compradores refused to buy verduras extrañas.

Some verbs need a person plus “to” plus infinitive. “Tell,” “persuade,” “encourage,” “warn,” and “remind” can work like this.

Algunos verbos necesitan una persona más “to” más infinitivo. “Tell,” “persuade,” “encourage,” “warn,” y “remind” pueden funcionar así.
  • Teachers encouraged families to visit the park.Los profesores encouraged a las familias to visit el parque.
  • Conservationists warned the council to protect the monkeys.Los conservacionistas warned al consejo to protect los monos.
  • The police told the teenagers to wait at the station.La policía told a los adolescentes to wait en la estación.

And some verbs are followed by verb plus “ing.” We can say:

Y algunos verbos van seguidos de verbo más “ing.” Podemos decir:
  • They suggested selling the fruit at lower prices.Ellos suggested selling la fruta a precios más bajos.
  • He admitted forgetting the backpack.Él admitted forgetting la mochila.
  • The police praised the teenagers for helping.La policía praised a los adolescentes for helping.
  • They blamed the company for damaging the forest.Ellos blamed a la compañía for damaging el bosque.

Direct Speech and Reported Speech

Let’s compare direct speech and reported speech.

Comparemos direct speech y reported speech.
  • Direct: “We will sell the fruit boxes tomorrow.” Reported: The buyers announced that they would sell the fruit boxes the next day.Direct: “We will sell the fruit boxes tomorrow.” Reported: Los compradores announced that venderían las fruit boxes al día siguiente.
  • Direct: “You should be careful.” Reported: The conservationists warned people to be careful.Direct: “You should be careful.” Reported: Los conservacionistas warned a la gente to be careful.
  • Direct: “Well done for calling us.” Reported: The police praised the teenagers for calling them.Direct: “Well done for calling us.” Reported: La policía praised a los adolescentes for calling them.

Pronunciation: Weak Syllables

Now let’s move to pronunciation. Today we practise weak syllables in reporting verbs, especially /ə/ and /ɪ/. In natural English, unstressed syllables are often short and weak.

Ahora pasemos a la pronunciación. Hoy practicamos weak syllables en reporting verbs, especialmente /ə/ y /ɪ/. En el inglés natural, las sílabas sin énfasis suelen ser cortas y débiles.

“Admit” is /ədˈmɪt/. The first sound is weak: /ə/. Say: admit, admit. “Announce” is /əˈnaʊns/. Again, weak /ə/ at the start.

“Admit” es /ədˈmɪt/. El primer sonido es débil: /ə/. Di: admit, admit. “Announce” es /əˈnaʊns/. Otra vez, /ə/ débil al inicio.

“Inform” is /ɪnˈfɔːm/. The first syllable is weak /ɪ/. “Report” is /rɪˈpɔːt/. “Persuade” is /pəˈsweɪd/. “Refuse” is /rɪˈfjuːz/. Listen: admit, announce, inform, report, persuade, refuse. The stress is on the second syllable.

“Inform” es /ɪnˈfɔːm/. La primera sílaba es débil /ɪ/. “Report” es /rɪˈpɔːt/. “Persuade” es /pəˈsweɪd/. “Refuse” es /rɪˈfjuːz/. Escucha: admit, announce, inform, report, persuade, refuse. El énfasis está en la segunda sílaba.

More practice: confirm, deny, remind, suggest. Confirm: /kənˈfɜːm/. Deny: /dɪˈnaɪ/. Remind: /rɪˈmaɪnd/. Suggest: /səˈdʒest/ or /səɡˈdʒest/. Keep the first syllable light and quick.

Más práctica: confirm, deny, remind, suggest. Confirm: /kənˈfɜːm/. Deny: /dɪˈnaɪ/. Remind: /rɪˈmaɪnd/. Suggest: /səˈdʒest/ o /səɡˈdʒest/. Mantén la primera sílaba ligera y rápida.

Otro ejemplo:

Review and Closing

Let’s review. Positive news can be inspiring, encouraging, uplifting, and remarkable. Negative news can be unfortunate, disturbing, or depressing. Reporting verbs make news more precise. We can say: announce that, assure someone that, promise to, encourage someone to, suggest doing, and praise someone for doing.

Repasemos. Positive news puede ser inspiring, encouraging, uplifting y remarkable. Negative news puede ser unfortunate, disturbing o depressing. Los reporting verbs hacen que las noticias sean más precisas. Podemos decir: announce that, assure someone that, promise to, encourage someone to, suggest doing, y praise someone for doing.
